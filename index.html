What these numbers mean · workforce

Reported employees, with the dates made clear.

Closest pre-transition snapshot

OPM reports month-end employment. December 31, 2024 is the last snapshot before Biden’s final full day, January 19, 2025—not an exact count for that day.

Latest available, not today’s payroll

September 30, 2026 is the latest OPM snapshot available as checked November 7. Some defense submissions are missing; non-defense data is unaffected according to OPM. August is also selectable.

Headcount ≠ effective service capacity

OPM Status counts employees in pay or non-pay status, across all work schedules. It does not establish who was actively working, hours delivered, employees on administrative leave, contractor capacity, or an FTE-adjusted effective workforce.

Coverage, missing records and interpretation

EHRI covers most executive-branch civilian employees, with exclusions including USPS, several intelligence agencies, Foreign Service personnel and other groups listed by OPM. It generally excludes military personnel and most legislative and judicial employees.

Net changes include hiring, exits, transfers, reorganizations and reporting differences. They are not counts of layoffs or vacancies, and do not establish a hiring target. An agency absent in either snapshot is marked missing rather than assigned zero. The overall figures sum all reported records in the chosen scope; agency comparisons require both observations.

September has approximately 10,000 missing defense Status records according to OPM’s release notes. We do not estimate or add those records back. Use the non-defense scope or August comparison to inspect sensitivity.

Funding and spending · source boundaries

Funding authority and spending uses answer different questions.

Reported authority

The topic view uses only reported discretionary authority, appropriations and payments from OMB SF 133 reports for FY2024 and FY2026. Mandatory funding and debt interest are excluded. They reflect funding enacted and adjusted during those years, including offsets and transfers. They are not presidential budget requests or a complete transcription of appropriation laws and their explanatory tables.

Topics across agencies

Accounts are mapped by OMB agency, bureau and account codes to an official purpose. Accounts without a unique match remain unclassified. The environment, energy and climate lens includes mixed-purpose accounts; it does not establish their climate-only spending allocation.

Actual uses of money

USAspending obligations compare the same October–August period in FY2024 and FY2026. Object classes describe uses such as personnel, grants and services; federal accounts identify the funded activities. These figures do not trace salary dollars or explain causes.
